What Is Coastal Erosion?

Coastal erosion is the gradual wearing away of land along the shoreline caused by waves, wind, currents and storms. It's a natural process, but it can speed up because of rising sea levels, more frequent storms, and human activity like removing dune plants or building too close to the coast.

At Piha, erosion affects the sand dunes, the cliffs around Lion Rock, and the native plants that hold the coastline together. Over time, this can mean less beach space, damaged habitats, and higher risk to buildings and tracks near the shore.

How You Can Help

Kaitiakitanga means taking responsibility for the places we care about — and looking after Piha starts with small, everyday choices. Stick to marked tracks and boardwalks instead of walking across dunes, since even a few footsteps can kill the plants holding the sand together. Take your rubbish home with you, join a local dune restoration or planting day if you get the chance, and speak up if you see others damaging the coastline. Protecting wildlife matters too — give nesting birds space, keep dogs on a leash near dune areas, and never disturb native plants. None of these actions take much effort on their own, but together they make a real difference in keeping Piha healthy for the generations who will visit after us.

"Take care of the land and the land will take care of you."

Kaitiakitanga reminds us that protecting nature today means protecting it for future generations.
